module 5

Phase Space Reconstruction of Core Body Temperature

One concept in the study of complex dynamical systems is the idea that we do not necessarily need to measure every single interacting variable to understand the system as a whole. According to Takens' (1981) Delay Embedding Theorem, the time series of a single variable inherently contains information about the other unmeasured variables within that same system. Because these variables dynamically couple and interact over time, taking delayed copies (lags) of our single measured variable allows us to create an isomorphic depiction of the entire system's underlying dynamics.

module 8

Measuring Feeding Coordination of Dairy Cows Using a Complex Systems Approach

In complex systems science, coordination is the emergence of order from the interaction of independent components. It governs phenomenons such as the synchronous flashing of fireflies, or the phase-locking of metronomes on a shared surface. Unlike centralized systems, where one controller dictates the movement of every part, coordinated systems rely on self-organization. The beauty of coordination lies in its ability to produce sophisticated, functional patterns that are often referred to as emergent properties. They are not present in the individual units themselves. In this paper, we aim to explore emerging coordination in the behavior of dairy cows.

module 10

Time-varying interaction strengths in dairy-cow thermoregulation

Dairy cows are a textbook state-dependent dynamical system: thermoregulation, behavior, and the herd's environmental state are nonlinearly coupled, and the strength of that coupling depends on which region of state space the animal currently occupies. We use S-Map (Sugihara 1994) on a multivariate embedding of CBT to estimate **time-varying Jacobian coefficients** — local linear sensitivities $\partial \mathrm{CBT}/\partial v$ for each predictor variable $v$ — and ask whether they reorganise across a documented heat-stress window. This is the slide-45 *"tracking interaction strength"* method applied to real physiological data.
